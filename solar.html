Questions people ask

How much solar do I need for my power station?

A good target is rated solar watts equal to about a third of the battery's watt-hours. A 1,000 Wh power station pairs well with about 300–350 W of panels, which refills it from empty in roughly one sunny day. Don't go past the station's own solar input limit, since anything above it is wasted.

Will any solar panel work with my power station?

Most of them, with the right adapter cable, but not all. Each station only accepts solar within a set voltage range: a panel above it can be refused or damage the station, and one below it won't charge. Extra watts are fine, since the station just takes what it can. Pick your station above and we'll only show panels that work with it.

Do panels really make their rated watts?

Rarely. Ratings are measured in lab conditions. In good real-world sun, expect about 70–80% of the rating, and much less in clouds, shade or winter. Our refill times assume 75%.

How we rank solar panels

Solar panels are simpler to compare than batteries: what you're buying is watts. So we rank by price per rated watt, lowest first. Size, weight and brand don't change the score. No brand pays to be listed or ranked.

When you pick a power station, we only show panels that work with its solar input, and we work out how many you need to reach about a third of the battery's watt-hours, without going over the station's input limit.
