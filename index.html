What do "W" and "Wh" mean?

Watts (W) are how much power something uses while it's on — like how fast water flows from a tap. A power station has a limit on how many watts it can supply at once, so it can't run a 1,500 W space heater if its limit is 1,000 W.

Watt-hours (Wh) are the size of the battery — like the size of the water tank. A 1,000 Wh battery can run a 100 W device for roughly 8–9 hours (a bit is lost along the way).

How do I find out how many watts my own stuff uses?

Look for a sticker or stamp on the back or bottom of the device, or on its power brick. It usually lists watts (W) directly. If it only shows volts (V) and amps (A), multiply them: 120 V × 2 A = 240 W. The numbers on this page are typical averages, so yours may be a little higher or lower.

Why do some things need so much more power to start?

Things with motors — fridges, freezers, pumps, air conditioners, power tools — briefly pull two to three times their normal power when they switch on. Most power stations can handle short bursts above their limit, but if you're running one of these, it's worth choosing a model with some room to spare.

What's mAh on a power bank?

It's another way of measuring battery size, used for small chargers. Bigger is more charges. A 10,000 mAh power bank charges a typical phone about twice; 20,000 mAh about four times.

Can I bring one on a plane?

Most power banks up to about 26,800 mAh (100 Wh) are allowed in your carry-on — never in checked bags. Power stations are almost always too big to fly. Check your airline's rules before you travel.

Does the battery type matter?

Look for LiFePO4 (sometimes written "LFP") in the description. These batteries last for thousands of charges and are very safe. Older or lighter models may use other types that wear out sooner.

How we decide what's a good value

We give each battery points for the things that matter: how much it holds, how many things you can plug in, whether it has enough power for what you need, and handy extras like a phone app. Then we compare that to what it typically costs. Batteries that give you more for your money rank higher — the most expensive one isn't automatically the best.

The value rating (from "Top value" down to "Pricey for what you get") shows where each one lands compared with everything else in the list. No brand pays to be listed or to change its ranking.

Show the exact formula

Capacity

Weighted on a curve (capacity0.585), so going from 500 Wh to 1,000 Wh counts for more than going from 2,000 Wh to 2,500 Wh.

Ports & outlets

Each wall outlet, 240V/high-amp outlet, and USB port adds a fixed amount.

Power output

Scored against the power you said you need. Extra power beyond that doesn't add to the score.

Extras

Expandable capacity, app support, jump-starting, MagSafe, built-in cables, a display, and a built-in wall plug each add a small fixed bonus.

Price

A typical selling price we check periodically is subtracted from the total. The value rating is based on where that result ranks within the category.

This is one opinionated formula, not an objective truth. Reasonable people could weigh things differently, so use the filters to see how the ranking shifts.
