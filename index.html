How value score is calculated

Most "best battery" lists quietly rank by whoever paid the most for premium marketing, or default to the most expensive option on the shelf. PowerRank does the opposite on purpose: price is one of the inputs to the score, not an afterthought. Every listed spec earns a unit points, then price is subtracted directly. A model with great specs at a high price and a model with modest specs at a low price can land in exactly the same place — that's the point. The score tells you where the balance falls, not just which one has the biggest number on the box.

Capacity

Weighted on a curve, not a straight line (capacity0.585) — so jumping from 500Wh to 1,000Wh is worth more to the score than jumping from 2,000Wh to 2,500Wh. That mirrors how capacity actually matters to most people: the first bump solves real problems, later bumps are diminishing returns for most use cases.

Ports & outlets

Each AC outlet, 120V/L2 port, and USB port adds a fixed amount, reflecting genuine day-to-day usefulness — more things you can plug in at once without an adapter.

Peak output

Scored relative to the "max power needed" slider, not in isolation. A unit only earns credit for headroom up to what you said you need — massive peak wattage you'll never use doesn't inflate the score.

Feature bonuses

Expandable capacity, companion app support, jump-starting, MagSafe, built-in cables, a display, and pass-through charging each add a modest, fixed bonus reflecting real convenience.

Price

Subtracted directly, in dollars, from everything above — so the final score is denominated the same way price is. A positive score means the spec sheet, in our formula's judgment, outweighs the price tag; negative means the price is outrunning what you're getting.

This is one opinionated formula, not an objective truth — the exact weights are judgment calls, and reasonable people could weigh capacity vs. ports vs. price differently depending on how they'll actually use a battery. Use the filters and sort options above to see the ranking shift as your own priorities change.
